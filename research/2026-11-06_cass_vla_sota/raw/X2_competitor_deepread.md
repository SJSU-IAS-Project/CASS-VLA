## Paper 1. RRDrive, arXiv:2609.21486 "Driving on Registers, Reasoning on Risk" (Chen et al., Tsinghua/HKUST-GZ/Neolix, cs.AI, 18 Sep 2026)
- Method: register-based end-to-end planner (generator + scorer, NAVSIM). A dense BEV "risk-aware occupancy" (RiskOcc) field is (a) fused into scene tokens to steer candidate generation and (b) sampled along each candidate trajectory ("ego footprint ... bilinearly samples the time-aligned map", top-k mean per channel, Sec. Risk-Aware Best-Candidate Selection) to build a per-candidate descriptor that augments the scorer heads.
- "Candidate-conditioned query" is a lookup into a dense field already computed; nothing is acquired, there is no tool choice, no abstain, no budget. The only "budget" is the number of candidates ("finite candidate budget").
- Headline (Abstract): selected-trajectory PDMS 0.951 vs baseline 0.937 (+1.5% rel.); avg candidate PDMS +7.7%; in challenging scenes candidate PDMS +30.2%; Spearman among good candidates 0.26 -> 0.67. Oracle-RiskOcc setting; external predictor reported as "competitive".
- Baseline for us: NO as a query selector. Could only serve as a "trajectory-conditioned feature" idea (sample a risk field along each candidate; our geometry features do similar). In MetaDrive: would need a BEV risk grid built from simulator state; easy to imitate as a feature, not a selector. Code: none found in text.
- Novelty risk: NONE for query selection; PARTIAL only for the phrase "trajectory-conditioned local query / candidate-specific risk profile".
- Collision quotes: "Trajectory-conditioned extraction samples local risks along each proposal and augments the scorer with explicit risk evidence." (Introduction). "candidate-conditioned risk queries support fine-grained selection" (Abstract).

## Paper 2. "When to Ask for Help: Uncertainty-Triggered Aerial Refinement for Off-Road Navigation", arXiv:2611.06702 (Samineni, Thorpe, ..., Topcu; cs.RO, 4 Nov 2026) -- NOT the same paper as #8
- Method: UGV with MPPI planner keeps navigation map layers (obstacle occupancy, semantic traversability, geometric) with uncertainty. When a navigation score S stays below threshold for N cycles ("degradation"), it holds the CURRENT MPPI rollout population fixed, replaces each uncertain layer by resolved hypotheses (obstacle layer: uncertain cells under rollout footprints set free / set occupied), re-scores the same rollouts, and defines per-layer counterfactual improvement Delta_l = max improvement. It requests the aerial measurement for the layer with the largest Delta_l above a threshold; if none passes, no request. After the UAV returns a map (obstacle map or semantic map), it fuses and replans. Semantic/geometric layers use a cheaper "rollout-conditioned exposure" (fraction of rollout footprint inside uncertain cells) instead of hypotheses.
- Fit with ours: this is the closest conceptual match found so far. It is a VOI-style, plan-set-conditioned choice among several acquisition actions (map layers) with abstain, rule-based (argmax over counterfactual improvement with thresholds), no learning.
- Quotes: "our method conditions assistance on whether resolving uncertainty improves predicted ground navigation" (Abstract). "We then select the corresponding UAV sensing action for the map layer with the largest useful improvement." (Introduction). "an optimistic form of value of information [22] applied to deciding when a robot should ask for help [23], with a UAV rather than a human as the helper." (Related Work). "This lets one navigation loop select among multiple aerial sensing roles and leaves the UAV idle when unresolved uncertainty does not constrain the current decision." (Related Work). Positioning against uncertainty-alone: "Uncertainty can be large in regions that have no effect on the current route, while localized uncertainty can prevent the UGV from identifying or executing a safe path." (Introduction).
- Results (Table I, 30 runs per direction x 3 routes = 180 trials per policy, Gazebo): goal reached Ground Only 167/180 (92.8%), UAV Counterfactual 178/180 (98.9%), Greedy 179/180 (99.4%), Explore-then-Drive 180/180. UAV flight: counterfactual 31.5 s mean vs 256.1 s greedy (abstract: "reducing mean UAV flight time by 87.7% relative to continuous greedy aerial mapping"). Navigation time saved per UAV second: 2.94 (CF), 0.95 (Explore then Drive), 0.47 (Greedy) (Fig. 4). Field test ~2 km; sim UAV time is "calculated analytically".
- Baselines: ground only, greedy always-on, explore-then-drive. NO random, NO scene-uncertainty-only trigger (despite the motivation), NO oracle, NO budget sweep / Pareto curve (single operating point per policy; thresholds fixed). Cost is reported as flight seconds, not as a swept budget.
- What we do that it does not: driving/camera-tool domain; a learned selector trained on expected-gain targets under 3 noise levels; explicit selector-vs-scene-uncertainty comparison at equal budget; Pareto sweep; oracle selector from privileged state; frozen VLM features.
- As baseline in MetaDrive: YES, and it is the best "rule-based counterfactual" baseline to add. Implementation: for each tool, build a resolved hypothesis for the uncertain element (hidden actor present/absent, light red/green, distance +-), re-run the ranker on the same K candidates, take max change in top-1 score or in argmax; pick tool with the largest change. Difficulty: moderate (needs the ranker to accept an altered state; with privileged simulator state we can do this in about a day). It is effectively our oracle-lite and must be cited.
- Novelty risk: PARTIAL to HIGH on the conceptual claim "choose the sensing action by its effect on the planner's current candidate set, not by uncertainty alone". Different domain (off-road UGV/UAV, map layers, MPPI, no learning, no budget curve).
- Code: project page https://anonymous.4open.science/w/when-to-ask-for-help/ (Abstract; anonymous, release status unknown).

## Paper 3. Belief-Conditioned One-Step Diffusion (B-COD), arXiv:2508.12166 (Puthumanaillam et al., UIUC/UF; cs.RO, v2 27 Aug 2025)
- Method: diffusion planner (distilled to one step, 10 ms) conditioned on a pose-belief raster, semantic map, goal and the sensor mask. It outputs a short-horizon trajectory with per-waypoint log-variance, condensed to a CVaR-95 localisation-risk scalar. A constrained SAC policy reads (belief raster, risk scalar, goal distance, previous mask) and toggles sensors on/off to minimize power subject to a risk-violation budget.
- The "spread" is the planner's own predicted aleatoric variance for its single output trajectory (a calibrated localisation-error proxy), NOT disagreement among K candidates. Sensor subset (5 modalities, any subset) is chosen by RL, conditioned on the planner via the scalar only.
- Quotes: "the spread of its denoising trajectories yields a calibrated, differentiable proxy for the expected localisation error." (Abstract). "We show that this single proxy suffices for a soft-actor-critic to choose sensors online, optimising energy while bounding pose-covariance growth." (Abstract). "B-COD reaches the goal on 97.9 % of attempts, yet spends only 42 % of the energy." (4.2 Key Finding #1).
- Table 1 (50 laps, real ASV): goal-reach AON 100.0, Greedy-OFF 47.3, InfoGain-Greedy 89.9, Random-1 18.5, Random-2 29.1, sigma-Mean 79.6, Sample-Spread 94.3, No-Belief 67.8, Pure-RL 54.8, DESPOT-Lite 87.9, B-COD 97.9. Energy vs AON: 100, 61.2, 49.8, 24.2, 38.9, 60.1, 91.2, 68.2, 67.5, 100, 42.3. Mean #sensors 5.0 ... B-COD 2.08.
- Baselines: always-on, rule heuristics, info-gain greedy, random-K, spread-based variants ("Sample-Spread" = 95th percentile of waypoint-wise Mahalanobis spread of 20 MC trajectory samples), pure RL, POMDP search. Budget: a per-step risk budget and a violation rate constraint, results at one setting (no sweep seen in the text I read). No oracle selector.
- Domain: marine localisation, sensors = power modes. Different problem (sensing for state estimation, not for choosing among candidate plans).
- Novelty risk: PARTIAL for "planner-generated trajectory spread drives a sensing decision"; but conditioning is on one trajectory's own uncertainty, per-sensor-power, and learned by constrained RL. Not candidate-set divergence and not a decision-accuracy objective. Our Sample-Spread-like baseline (spread of K candidates -> pick tool) is a natural analogue of their "Sample-Spread" ablation; cite it.
- As baseline in MetaDrive: only the "spread" feature (cheap, hours). The SAC/diffusion stack is not worth porting.
- Code: https://github.com/bcod-diffusion/bcod ; dataset https://github.com/bcod-diffusion/dataset ; website bcod-diffusion.github.io (Appendix "Project resources").

## Paper 4. Risk-Adaptive Edge-Cloud Visual Reasoning, arXiv:2608.14991 (cs.RO, 2026)
- Method: onboard Qwen3-VL-4B + YOLO11n produce structured traffic evidence and an urgency level (no hazard / caution / stop / blocked). The cloud VLM (gpt-5-mini) is called on startup, on an urgency increase ("semantic event"), after a 3 s cooldown for persistent hazards, and a 10 s audit. Cloud returns a tactical maneuver; local validation and AEB retain authority. CARLA 0.9.15, Town04.
- Decision is binary (call cloud or not), scene-state based, rule-based, trajectory-agnostic. Does not pick among tools or candidates.
- Results: "Risk-adaptive used 54.1% fewer cloud requests, 51.9% less image payload, and 51.2% fewer tokens than Periodic-cloud" (V-A, Table III: 399 vs 183 calls). Task success 40/40 for both; strict success (no AEB) 35/40 vs 29/40; Edge-only 30/40. RQ3: both 47/60 in aggregate under network profiles; Risk-adaptive 1.92-3.67 s slower lane change.
- Baselines: edge-only, periodic cloud, audit-only. No random, no oracle, no sweep (fixed 3 s / 10 s intervals).
- Novelty risk: NONE for candidate conditioning; it is the type of trajectory-agnostic scene-urgency trigger our selector is supposed to beat (same family as DriveAgent-R1 baseline). Weak baseline: periodic vs event-triggered.
- MetaDrive baseline: trivial as a scene-uncertainty/urgency trigger (already our "scene-uncertainty" baseline). Code: not stated in the text.

## Paper 5. When to Probe and When to Act, arXiv:2611.02240 (Wang, Chakravarty, Lyu; cs.RO, 1 Nov 2026)
- Method: belief-space MPC over a latent human-driver characteristic. A scalar "clarity" derived from the Riccati covariance is a planning variable; low clarity both rewards probing actions (clarity penalty as surrogate for multistep info gain) and tightens a CVaR barrier. Probing is an ego MOTION (e.g., cautious forward probe), not a perception-tool call. No tool choice, no candidate set, no learning.
- Results (Abstract): over 5,000 simulations, success 94.6% (lane merge) and 93.2% (unsignalized intersection), collisions 1.2% and 1.4% "among the compared methods". Baselines: RA-MPPI, AIG-MPC, D-MPPI, ID-SMPC (500 runs per method per scenario). No budget curve.
- Novelty risk: NONE. Useful only as related work for "when to probe vs act" and the diminishing-returns argument (Proposition 1: probing cost bounded by residual ambiguity). Not a baseline. Code: none stated.

## Paper 6. UDAV, arXiv:2609.16368 (cs.RO, 14 Sep 2026; UAV-guided UGV off-road)
- Method: fine-tuned local VLM maps an aerial image + start + target to 16 image-space waypoints. K stochastic samples; medoid is the nominal path; per-waypoint covariance across the samples is the uncertainty; if the max normalized interior-waypoint uncertainty exceeds a threshold the VLM is re-prompted ("reconsideration") with the disagreement region (optionally a high-res crop), and the final path is the medoid of the reconsidered samples. Evaluation on GA3T dataset, 400 held-out queries.
- Quotes: "we ask whether disagreement among stochastic trajectory predictions can serve as an actionable signal for when an otherwise self-consistent plan should be reconsidered." (Introduction). "UDAV ... a mean ADE of 110.4 px, a 25.1% reduction, with valid trajectories for all 400 queries" vs 147.4 px deterministic and 115.9 px medoid (Abstract). Reconsideration activated on 174 of 400 queries (ADE 153.7 -> 141.0 px). Crop ablation (IV-D): no extra image 144.6 px, broad crop 150.3 px, peak-local crop 138.0 px, "neither crop variant is significantly different".
- The spread is among samples of ONE generator for the SAME goal (epistemic self-consistency of a VLM), not among semantically different candidate plans; the action is "think again", not "which tool". Trajectory-aware (it uses the trajectory dispersion), but a single trigger, no choice among tools, no budget sweep (one threshold plus a higher-compute consensus baseline).
- Novelty risk: PARTIAL, only for "gate extra compute on trajectory spread". Not a candidate-set / tool-choice method.
- Baseline in MetaDrive: the spread-gate is easy (hours) as "trajectory-spread trigger, tool-agnostic", a useful ablation separating "spread" from "which tool". Code: "reproducibility package" with annotations and fine-tuned weights, link not captured from text.

## Paper 7. Auditing Latent-Space Monitors for Autonomous Driving, arXiv:2609.30557 (arXiv preprint, 2026-09 by id; category not checked)
- Method: audit of failure monitors (density GMM and supervised probes) on frozen LaneSegNet (OpenLane-V2) and VAD (nuScenes), asking whether latents add predictive value over non-latent inputs (ego state, command, predicted trajectory, prediction output).
- Finding (Abstract): "Adding latent features to either baseline yields no statistically resolved improvement." and "predicting failure from an internal representation does not establish that the representation provides useful information beyond observable inputs and outputs." Table 2 (VAD, AUROC, ADE endpoint): planning latent .868; ego+trajectory+command .924; latent + non-latent .917.
- No query selection, no tools, no budget; evaluated by AUROC/AP and selective-risk. Novelty risk: NONE. Relevance to us: (a) a protocol warning for our VLM-embedding result (+0.3 to +0.7 points): include a strong non-latent baseline (geometry features, which we already have) and test incremental value with CIs; (b) the planned trajectory as input is a strong failure predictor, which supports using candidate geometry; (c) future calibration phase related work (RiskMonitor, REDOUBT, Argus named in its Related Work). As baseline: no. Code: releases per-frame labels for 61,432 samples (text does not give a link).

## Paper 8. "When to Ask for Help: A Decision-Theoretic Account and Measurement Standard for Slow-Module Scheduling in Dual-System VLA Models", arXiv:2610.21160 (Oct 2026) -- NOT paper 2
- Method: formalizes slow-module invocation as a calibrated decision under a query budget; Value of Intervention (VoI) = expected gap between slow-corrected and fast-only value, weighted by probability the fast policy collapses; fixed-ratio, fixed-budget and history-gated schedules are degenerate cases (Props 1-3); renewal baseline: "under state-independent collapse the optimal schedule is periodic"; router estimates VoI and queries "only when the estimate exceeds the query cost"; four-measure leak-free evaluation protocol (staleness curves, routing frontiers, failure attribution, calibration reliability).
- Evidence: synthetic testbed with matched budgets ("Pareto-dominates all three baseline families"; the success numbers were stripped by my math filter, so UNVERIFIED here); 929 RoboICL traces; FiS-VLA on an RTX 4090; closed-loop LIBERO arms with a negative result: "the language-rewrite channel cannot convert them into success on either of two task suites".
- Collisions with ours: the framing "decision problem under a query budget", "fixed-ratio ... degenerate cases of one quantity", the Pareto frontier at matched budget, and a leak-free rule. All are whether-to-call (binary), state-conditioned, not candidate-conditioned, no tool choice. Its Future work lists "Mixture routing. Routing over intervention types ..., testing whether type selection Pareto-dominates any single type." -- the "which" question is explicitly open there, which supports our contribution.
- Useful for us: (1) predicts that adaptive triggers gain only when collapse is heterogeneous: "the calibrated advantage over the fixed cadence vanishes under homogeneous collapse and grows monotonically with collapse heterogeneity"; we should report that our selector's gain over random/always grows with hazard heterogeneity (we have 3 hazard types and noise levels). (2) The leak-free rule: selector features must be available before the tool call; our "expected gain" targets must not leak oracle fields into features. (3) Cluster/design-effect warning: bootstrap by scenario/episode, not by decision step.
- Novelty risk: PARTIAL on the budgeted-VoI framing; NONE on candidate conditioning or tool choice. Baseline in MetaDrive: its VoI router is a "when" gate; a threshold on predicted collapse probability can be added to our always-on/none comparison cheaply. Code: not stated in the text.

## Paper 9. When Should a VLM Look? CounterCredit, arXiv:2609.22910 (Sep 2026)
- Method: RL (dual-channel GRPO) for image-QA agents with a crop tool. For each call, from the realized pre-call prefix, three continuations: answer now, real crop, random same-size patches. Decision value = gold-answer score of the real branch minus answer-now; evidence value = real minus random patches. A call earns "cashback" only if both exceed a deadzone on a correct trajectory; all other executed calls pay "rent".
- Numbers: "On our cold-start checkpoint only 10% to 12% of visual calls were both needed and used, and released agents make spurious calls 36% to 87% of the time" (Abstract); CounterCredit "89.5% on V*, 80.2% on HR-Bench-4K, and 76.4% on HR-Bench-8K, 6.3 to 9.4 points above outcome-only GRPO at 1.78 against 1.84 calls per question" and "lowers the spurious-call rate to 31% to 36%".
- Single tool (crop), uses gold answer (training-time only), no candidate plans, no budget sweep (calls per question reported at the end). Novelty risk: NONE for our claim; it is relevant as (a) the "decision value" notion = our expected-gain label per tool, (b) the evidence-value null (substitute random patches) = a placebo control we could add: a "random-ROI" tool result to test that gains come from content, not merely from calling a tool. Baseline in MetaDrive: no. Code: not stated in the text.

## Cross-paper observations
- No paper among these chooses WHICH perception tool to call conditioned on a set of candidate trajectories in driving. Closest is #2 (UAV, rule-based, counterfactual rollouts, no budget curve, no scene-uncertainty/random baselines), followed by #3 (single-trajectory uncertainty drives sensor subset) and #8 (explicitly leaves type routing as future work).
- #2 is the paper to cite and to reimplement as a "counterfactual-rollout" rule baseline (see Paper 2 for the recipe).
- Possible evaluation borrowings: matched-budget Pareto with CIs (#8), flight-seconds-saved-per-query-second efficiency ratio (#2: "2.94"), placebo/random-observation control (#9), incremental-value test over a strong non-latent baseline (#7).
- Caveats: all text is from arXiv HTML; math was stripped, so equation symbols and some numbers in #8 and #7 abstracts (AUROC values) were not recoverable from my strip and are marked above or taken from tables. I did not read the experiments of #1 beyond the abstract-level numbers and Section 3 text, nor all of #3's appendices.

## Novelty statement
To our reading of nine competitor papers' full text, no prior work selects among several perception tools (ROI inspection, depth, collision check) for a driving planner conditioned on the divergence of K candidate trajectories, and none evaluates that choice as decision accuracy versus query budget against scene-uncertainty, random and oracle selectors. The nearest method, "When to Ask for Help: Uncertainty-Triggered Aerial Refinement" (2611.06702), rescales a fixed rollout set under counterfactual map hypotheses to pick an aerial measurement, but it is off-road, rule-based, evaluated at single operating points without scene-uncertainty or random baselines, and should be cited as the closest conceptual precedent and reimplemented as a baseline. B-COD (2508.12166) and UDAV (2609.16368) use trajectory spread, but of a single planner output or of one VLM's samples, and gate sensor power or a second VLM pass rather than choose a query. Papers 4, 5, 8 and 9 address whether to call, probe or look and are trajectory-agnostic; #8 even lists type routing as open future work. The claim should therefore be worded as "candidate-set-conditioned choice among perception tools in driving, with a budget Pareto", not as the first use of plan-conditioned value of information.
