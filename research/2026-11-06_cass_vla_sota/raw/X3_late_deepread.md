## 1. Calibrate-Then-Delegate (CTD), arXiv:2604.14251 (cs.LG, v2 21 Aug 2026; Pona et al., KCL/Manchester/Northeastern London) -- full HTML read
Domain: LLM safety monitoring (not driving, not robotics). Code: none stated in the text.

Exact method. Cheap latent safety probe on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct layer-11 activations scores every input; an expensive expert (Gemma-3-27B-IT "strong", Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct "weak") is called only when a second probe, the "delegation value (DV) probe", exceeds a threshold. DV label = (correct-class probability of expert) minus (correct-class probability of probe) ("where p_probe and p_expert are the probabilities assigned to the correct class by the probe and expert"; Sec. Delegation-Value Probes), computed offline from both models' outputs and the true label; DV probe is regressed on it with squared loss. Threshold is calibrated on held-out data with Learn-then-Test (fixed-sequence testing) plus Pareto filtering, in two modes: guarantee expert-call budget while optimising accuracy/AUROC, or guarantee accuracy while minimising calls. Binary decision (call or not), instance-level, one expert. 20 budget levels swept.

Baselines (all present): uncertainty (proximity-to-median) calibrated, uncertainty top-k (McKenzie et al. 2025), DV top-k, and "Oracle top-k" using ground-truth DV. No random baseline in the text I read.

Headline numbers (verbatim, Sec. Overall Cascade Performance): "At the representative budget alpha_B=0.35, CTD improves AUROC by +4.0 points and accuracy by +2.8 points, averaged over three seeds." "DV top-k exceeds uncertainty top-k by +3.8 AUROC points and +2.8 accuracy points." Performance control: "CTD has an unconditional violation rate of 5.6%, below the target, ... the empirical selector violates the target in 43.6% of trials. CTD returns a policy in 66.0% of trials". Weak expert: "the uncertainty top-k baseline therefore invokes the expert on 80% of inputs, while CTD uses roughly 35% and achieves higher AUROC and accuracy". Budget guarantee check: "budget-only LTT violates the target in 8.6% of strong-expert trials and 6.6% of weak-expert trials".

What it shares with us (novelty risk: PARTIAL, on the training-label recipe and the calibration phase; NONE on the core claim).
- Value signal is the realized gain of the expensive call vs. the cheap one, learned by a lightweight head, and compared against an uncertainty trigger: "uncertainty is a poor proxy for the utility of an expert call, as it ignores whether the expert would actually improve the prediction" (Abstract). This is the same argument as our expected-gain target vs. scene-uncertainty.
- Budget sweep with an oracle (ground-truth DV) top-k as the upper bound; same plot shape as our Pareto.
- Conformal-style calibration (LTT) of the trigger threshold with finite-sample guarantee on budget or accuracy: this is exactly our "future phase" (conformal risk control). We cannot call calibrated thresholding of a learned value signal new; it is prior work, though in an LLM cascade.
- Harmful calls: "overwriting the probe score with the expert score may also harm safety" (Introduction); negative DV subsets.
What ours does that it does not: tool choice among heterogeneous queries (it has one expert); conditioning on candidate plans (its input is the probe's own latent, no action set); perception/driving; tool-noise levels; hazards; ranker with oracle best candidate; a random baseline.
Baseline in MetaDrive: as a method, yes and cheap. The DV-style "predicted benefit of the call, thresholded" is our MLP selector's value head already; what to add is (a) an LTT-calibrated threshold on the predicted gain (about half a day with the existing expected-gain table, since the labels are per-tool gains) and (b) its "Unc. calibrated" vs "DV calibrated" comparison. Not a baseline for candidate conditioning.
Quotes that collide with our claims: "uncertainty is a poor proxy for the utility of an expert call, as it ignores whether the expert would actually improve the prediction" (Abstract); "We then propose DV probes, latent probes trained to predict this quantity, yielding a more informative and target-aligned delegation signal" (Introduction); "CTD calibrates a threshold on the DV signal using held-out data and multiple hypothesis testing, yielding finite-sample guarantees" (Abstract).

## 2. To Call or Not to Call, arXiv:2605.00737 (cs.AI, v3 6 Aug 2026; Wu et al., MPI-SWS) -- full HTML read
Domain: LLM tool calling (web search, calculator). Code: "Code and dataset available at https://github.com/QinyuanWu0710/ToCall_or_NotToCall" (footnote on Abstract).

Exact method. Framework of necessity / utility / affordability. Labels come from counterfactual runs: No Tool vs Always Tool. True utility "Delta*(x) = s_AT(x) - s_NT(x)"; true need = No-Tool score below an acceptable-quality threshold. Under a budget of K calls "the optimal allocation selects the K largest positive gains". Learned part: lightweight MLP on the model's final-token last-layer hidden state: Latent Need Estimator (LNE, predicts true need, hidden state taken under No Tool) and Latent Utility Estimators (LUE, predict positive utility; one variant also sees a tool description). Budgeted allocation: rank instances by LNE/LUE confidence, give calls to the top K, score by realized true gain. Single binary decision per tool; the framework is run per tool (two tools, six tasks), the controller does not choose among tools. 7 models (3B to 120B + GPT-5.5).

Baselines: Self-decision (model decides), No Tool, Always Tool, Optimal (hindsight oracle); AdaptiveRAG compared in appendix E.3.3. No random baseline in the main text. Budgets 20/40/80% of calls plus natural rate.

Headline numbers (verbatim): Table 1 (Entity task, GPT-OSS-120B): No Tool 0.61 (0), Always Tool 0.76 (100), Self-decision 0.72 (30), Optimal 0.81 (61) (score, call rate %). "when No Tool performance is High, 32% instances degrade", "58% instances ... lie on the diagonal, suggesting that tool calling is often redundant" (Sec. 4.1). LNE vs Self at 20% budget, Entity: Qwen3-30B-IT 0.73 vs 0.71, Llama3.2-3B 0.62 vs 0.61, Gemma3-27B 0.66 vs 0.63; but GPT-OSS-120B 0.66 vs 0.68 (LNE worse) and Mistral GSM-Hard 0.43 vs 0.51 natural (Table 2). Authors state: "LUEs do not reliably predict the true utility, leaving utility estimation as a key open challenge" (Intro) and "utility estimation remains substantially harder than need estimation, and utility-based controllers exhibit small improvement over Self-decision" (Sec. 4.3).

Overlap with ours (risk: PARTIAL on framing; NONE on method).
- Same decomposition of a query's value into counterfactual gain labels (with-tool minus without-tool), budget as top-K by gain, oracle = K largest gains, and the observation that calls can hurt ("Tool calls can hurt performance when there is no true need"). Our expected-gain targets and oracle selector are the same construct in a different domain.
- Learned lightweight head on frozen model features (hidden state) trained on those labels, evaluated at fixed budgets: parallels our MLP + frozen Qwen3.5 embeddings.
- Important negative result for us: "Need depends only on the model and task and is readable from hidden states; utility additionally depends on what the tool will return and how well the model will use it." Our setting is the utility case (which tool will reveal what); their utility estimator fails. This is a warning that learned per-tool gain may be hard from VLM embeddings alone, and an argument that candidate-geometry features (which tell what the tool would reveal) are what is missing in their LUE.
What ours does that it does not: choice among several tools in one decision; candidate-plan conditioning; perception tools; hazard injection and noise control; privileged-state oracle for decision quality; Pareto against scene-uncertainty.
Baseline in MetaDrive: the "Need-only" estimator is our scene-uncertainty baseline in spirit (predicts if the base model fails without the tool, ignoring tool); easy (hours): train a head to predict "ranker without tool is wrong" and rank by it. The oracle-by-top-K-gain is already our oracle.
Quotes that collide: "we posit that the optimal goal of tool calling should be to help the model maximize the expected improvement in task performance (utility gain) afforded by tool use, under cost constraints" (Introduction); "Under a budget of at most K calls, the optimal allocation selects the K largest positive gains." (Sec. 3, Normative lens).

## 3. Active Sensing and Deferred-Decision Trajectory Optimization (AS-DDTO), arXiv:2606.22277 (eess.SY, 21 Jun 2026; Siahkali, Hou, Gupta; Purdue/Notre Dame) -- full HTML read
Domain: mobile sensor (quadrotor) identifying which of N=4 candidate TARGET locations is real. Code: none stated.

Exact method. Deferred-Decision Trajectory Optimization (DDTO) plans one trajectory per candidate target that stay coincident (shared "trunk") as long as possible. AS-DDTO adds a weighted information-acquisition term (w=40) that rewards the shared trunk for being close to candidates because "measurements whose informativeness varies with the distance between the sensor and a candidate" (Sec. III). The sensing budget is a per-step query limit: "the sensor queries Q_k subset of J with |Q_k| <= L" (L=1 Gaussian, L=2 conformal). Two belief models: Bayesian Gaussian and a conformal candidate set (Fisher-combined p-values). It is a mixed-integer conic optimisation, hand-designed, no learning of the trigger (the only learned part is a 16-d measurement MLP for the conformal score). The "candidates" are goal hypotheses, not alternative plans for one goal.
Baselines: only DDTO (w=0), offline and online variants. No random, uncertainty trigger or oracle. Budget: L=2 vs L=3 sensitivity only ("increasing the query budget to L=3 gave 66% interception"), no Pareto.
Headline (verbatim, Tables I-II, 100 runs): Gaussian online interception DDTO 33%, AS-DDTO(d^2) 53%, ID rate 33% vs 58%. Conformal: "Online AS-DDTO improves interception over DDTO (61% vs. 53%)"; "A more conservative retained set with alpha=0.05 increased interception to 71%".
Overlap (risk: NONE to low). It shares only the vocabulary "trajectory-dependent information acquisition under a sensing budget". The trajectory affects what you measure through geometry (distance to targets), the decision is where to go, not which tool to call, and the candidate set is of target identities. Its conformal use is for belief sets with fixed-time coverage, which is relevant background for our conformal phase only.
What ours does that it does not: tool selection, candidate-plan conditioning for a fixed goal, learning, driving perception, budget Pareto, any baseline beyond its own ablation.
Baseline in MetaDrive: no (continuous-optimisation target-identification problem, not transferable). Do not spend time.
Quotes: "adding a trajectory-dependent information-acquisition term to the planning objective" (Abstract); "Existing DDTO formulations are information-passive: they assume that the target identity is revealed through an exogenous process" (Introduction).

## 4. Perception in Plan (VeteranAD), arXiv:2508.11488 (cs.CV, 15 Aug 2025; Zhang et al., Fudan) -- full HTML read
Domain: end-to-end driving, NAVSIM navtest and Bench2Drive (CARLA). Code: github.com/fudan-zvg/VeteranAD (given on the HTML page header, not opened).

Exact method. A network design, not a query policy. Multi-mode anchored trajectories (planning priors) initialise planning queries. "The Planning-Aware Holistic Perception module takes the planning queries and performs cross-attention with image features, BEV features, and surrounding agent features, guided by the points on the anchored trajectories" (Fig. 2 caption). "Localized Autoregressive Trajectory Planning" then adjusts each trajectory step by step, "perform[ing] perception at each time step to generate the corresponding planning waypoint". So attention is conditioned on each candidate trajectory's own points: "the perception module is specifically designed to gather traffic elements along these trajectories" (Abstract). Everything is always on: no budget, no choice of tool, no abstain, learned end to end by imitation of expert plans.
Headline (verbatim, Comparison with state of the art): "VeteranAD achieves a PDM Score (PDMS) of 90.2"; "surpasses UniAD by 6.8 PDMS" on NAVSIM navtest; Bench2Drive "average L2 distance of 0.60 in the open-loop evaluation"; closed-loop "on par with ... DriveTransformer and DriveAdapter", and "still achieves a relatively low success rate in closed-loop evaluation" (Appendix, Limitations).
Table columns: acquired = feature gathering (cross-attention reads) along each anchor; choice among tools: no; candidate-conditioned: yes (anchor-conditioned feature sampling); value signal: none (end-to-end imitation loss); learned; budget: no; baselines: other E2E planners (UniAD, DiffusionDrive, WoTE ...); oracle: no.
Overlap (risk: PARTIAL, vocabulary only). It is the nearest published statement of the idea that perception should be "guided by evolving planning objectives". Reviewers may cite it as "trajectory-conditioned perception". Differences are fundamental: it conditions what features are read, for free, inside the network, whereas we decide whether to pay for an external tool call and which one, at a budget.
What ours does that it does not: explicit query/abstain decision, heterogeneous tools, cost, privileged-state oracle, hazard-injected scenarios.
Baseline in MetaDrive: no as a method (BEV/multi-view E2E network, NAVSIM/Bench2Drive training). Cite as related work; its anchor-conditioned attention is a possible architectural idea for the selector (attend over candidate waypoints).
Quote that collides: "This design facilitates targeted perception guided by evolving planning objectives over time" (Abstract); "the perception module operates in a targeted manner, aligned with the requirements of the planning process" (Introduction).

## 5. Active Probing with Multimodal Predictions for Motion Planning, arXiv:2507.09822 (cs.RO, v4 22 Jul 2025; Gadginmath et al., UC Riverside/Honda RI) -- full HTML read
Domain: MetaDrive lane-change merge (N=3 vehicles) and unsignalized intersection (N=7). Code: "Code and videos are available at https://darshangm.github.io/papers/active-probing-multimodal-predictions/" (Abstract; not opened).

Exact method. MPC objective = utility + expected safety + alpha_3 * sum_i Info(phi_i, x_e, u_e). The "probing" is an ego motion (accelerate, steer) chosen so other drivers' reaction reveals their behaviour parameters phi_i (aggressive vs defensive); belief over phi updated through a modified Boltzmann model of the other agent's reward. A Wasserstein-based risk metric over Gaussian-mixture predictions. No perception tool, no learning of the information value, no budget.
Baselines: CC-MPC (chance-constrained MPC with multimodal predictions, no probing) and own variant with probing off. No random, uncertainty trigger or oracle.
Headline (verbatim Table II, 200 Monte Carlo lane-change episodes): success CC-MPC 62%, no probing 82%, probing 98%; time to merge 9.271 / 7.215 / 6.871 s. Intersection (Table III): success 89% / 93% / 96%, collision rate 3% / 0.5% / 0.0%.
Overlap (risk: NONE for query selection). It is a MetaDrive paper that uses "action to gain information" and so is closest to us in simulator only. Information is gained by changing the ego motion (candidate action influences observation), which is a trajectory-conditioned information term, but the target is other-agent intent not perception-tool output.
What ours does that it does not: tool/abstain choice, equal-budget comparison, learned selector, hazard injection.
Baseline in MetaDrive: no. Conceptual note only: it shows MetaDrive's built-in IDM/RL agents are accepted as test traffic with behaviour parameters, which we can cite as precedent for our scenario construction.
Quote: "an active probing mechanism that strategically selects actions to improve its estimates of behavioral parameters of other agents" (Abstract).

## 6. PRAM-R, arXiv:2603.04222 (cs.RO, 4 Mar 2026; Zhang et al., TUM) -- full HTML read
Domain: nuScenes (offline logs) plus synthetic stress tests. Code: none stated in the text.

Exact method. Qwen3-VL-8B is an "LLM router" that, from camera brightness/contrast/edge-density, LiDAR density/noise/reflectivity and radar diagnostics (Table I) plus scene description, outputs JSON with "per-modality reliability scores in [0,1] for camera, LiDAR, and radar, ... a scalar scene complexity score ... and binary modality usage indicators" (Sec. IV-A3). Hysteresis + EMA stabilise switching; hierarchical memory caches routing decisions; slow deliberative loop runs at 1 Hz. The router chooses among sensor modalities (camera/LiDAR/radar), which is a choice among several, but it is conditioned on sensor health and scene context, not on candidate plans. Prompted, not trained; no value label.
Baseline: only "PLA", all modalities active. No random, no uncertainty trigger, no oracle, no sweep.
Headline (verbatim, Abstract): "Synthetic stress tests confirm 87.2% reduction in routing oscillations via hysteresis-based stabilization. Real-world validation on nuScenes shows 6.22% modality reduction with 20% memory recall while maintaining comparable trajectory accuracy to full-modality baselines". Table IV: Static Fusion ADE 1.013 / FDE 2.026; Full PRAM-R RE 6.22%, ADE 1.124 / FDE 2.182, i.e. about 11% worse ADE despite "comparable"; evaluated on "10 representative scenes".
Overlap (risk: NONE-to-low). A VLM-guided sensor-selection-for-driving paper, so reviewers may group it with ours, but nothing is decision-relevant or plan-conditioned and the effect is tiny (6.22% sensors off, accuracy slightly worse).
Ours that it lacks: candidate conditioning, learned value, accuracy-vs-budget evaluation, strong baselines.
Baseline in MetaDrive: weak; a rule router (scene-complexity -> turn tool on) is the same as our scene-uncertainty baseline. Skip.
Quote: "An LLM router selects and weights modalities using environmental context and sensor diagnostics" (Abstract).

## 7. DeeAD, arXiv:2511.20720 (cs.CV, 25 Nov 2025; Hu et al., CityU HK) -- full HTML read
Domain: VLA (ORION) on Bench2Drive open-loop. Code: none stated in the text.

Exact method. Training-free early exit across decoder layers. An "Early Exit Action Head" decodes an intermediate trajectory at layer l; stop when the L2 between it and a navigation/low-precision-planning prior is within tolerance delta ("terminates inference when predicted trajectories align with lightweight planning priors (e.g., Navigation or Low-precision Planning) within a tolerable deviation (<= 2m)"); a "Multi-Hop Exit Controller" skips which layers to check. What is conserved is compute inside one model, not a perception query; trajectory-conditioned only in the sense that the stop rule compares the current plan with a prior. Rule-based, delta is a knob.
Baselines: vanilla ORION and "Fixed-EE" (confidence-based). Budget sweep: delta in {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} m gives three operating points. No random or oracle.
Headline (verbatim, Sec. 4.2): "delta=0.5 m ... skips 21.3% of layers ... reduces latency from 381 ms to 322 ms ... average L2 error drops from 0.67 m to 0.58 m and the average collision rate decreases from 0.47% to 0.42%"; "delta=2.0 m ... skips 28.0% of layers and achieves 270 ms latency (roughly 29% faster than ORION) ... average L2 (1.13 m), yet the collision rate (0.45%)"; Fixed-EE "Avg. L2 = 1.63 m ... collisions (1.32%)". Note "deeper layers do not universally guarantee improvement" (Sec. 2.3), an echo of the "more evidence can hurt" finding.
Overlap (risk: NONE). Different resource (layers), different decision (stop thinking).
Baseline in MetaDrive: no. Possible borrow: a compute-saving "stop when the top-1 candidate is stable" rule as an extra trajectory-aware trigger, hours of work, low value.
Quote: "Instead of relying on confidence scores, DeeAD terminates inference when predicted trajectories align with lightweight planning priors" (Abstract).

## 8. Proof-of-Perception (PoP), arXiv:2603.00324 (Fayyazi, Akrami; USC/Nuro) -- full HTML read
Domain: document/chart/multi-image QA (DocVQA, TextVQA, InfographicVQA, ChartQA-H, MultiDoc2Dial, TextCaps), not driving. Code: github.com/AryaFayyazi/PoP (Abstract).

Exact method. An MLLM planner emits a DAG with `CALL_TOOL(k, region, prompt)` nodes (OCR, detector, chart parser, layout parser) and fuse nodes. Each node has a learned nonconformity score and a split-conformal threshold giving a set Gamma. A controller "pi_phi" reads the certificate state (threshold, set size, node type), a global budget and context, and picks from {ACCEPT, RETRY, EXPAND, ABORT}. Controller is trained by policy gradient with "reward R = -C_err - beta C_comp (beta = 0.05)". Budget: per-sample B = 16; a tool call costs 1, high-res retry 2, fuse step 0.25. So: choice among several tools (the planner chooses which tool, via the DSL; the controller decides whether to expand), learned, conformal-set uncertainty as the trigger signal, budget sweep B in {8,12,16,24} with accuracy-compute frontiers.
Baselines: Direct-MLLM, M-CoT, MM-ReAct, ProgVLM, two 2025 planner-tools-fuser agents. No random or oracle selector; the uncertainty-driven controller is itself the "scene-uncertainty" family (conformal set size).
Headline (verbatim, Table 1, DocVQA-T1 EM): Direct 69.8, M-CoT 72.1, MM-ReAct 74.3, ProgVLM 75.0, PoP 78.6; hallucination 7.1; budget Bbar 11.2 vs 14.8 (MM-ReAct). Sec. 4.7: "at B=12 it matches or exceeds the best baseline at B=16 on all datasets, saving 25% compute". Ablation: "heuristic controller" (retry twice) "increases average budget by +18% at similar EM". Coverage "90.7% +- 0.9 (ocr-string)". Caveat: the baseline numbers appear to be the authors' own runs under "matched" settings; only a few deltas per ablation; no standard deviations for Table 1.
Overlap (risk: PARTIAL on "budgeted selection among perception tools with conformal certificates", NONE on candidate-plan conditioning or driving). It is the most complete "tool choice + conformal + budget Pareto" precedent, but the trigger is the node's own uncertainty, which is exactly the family our paper argues against, and there is no trajectory/action set.
Ours that it lacks: conditioning on candidate actions, decision-accuracy objective (it optimises answer EM), oracle selector, tool-noise sweep, the explicit comparison of scene-uncertainty vs conditioned trigger.
Baseline in MetaDrive: partial and costly (needs a conformal head per tool, a trained controller); we can port the idea cheaply as the "conformal-set-size trigger" (set size of the ranker over K candidates; call a tool when the conformal set has more than one candidate): about one day. That is a good, defensible "certified uncertainty" baseline and it previews our future conformal phase.
Quotes that collide: "a lightweight controller uses these certificates to allocate compute under a budget--expanding with extra tool calls only when needed and stopping early otherwise" (Abstract); "turning uncertainty into a compute policy rather than a passive score" (Introduction).

## 9. Combining Cost-Constrained Runtime Monitors for AI Safety, arXiv:2507.15886 (cs.CY, v4 21 Oct 2025; Hua et al., MARS/Redwood) -- full HTML read
Domain: LLM-output monitoring (BigCodeBench backdoors, Llama 3.1 8B/70B monitors). Code: github.com/tim-hua-01/combining_monitors_public (Introduction).

Exact method. Objective: maximise recall of misaligned outputs under one unified average-case budget covering monitor calls and audits. Policy = (a) "when and which monitors to call" chosen by exhaustive search over a defined class of invocation strategies (feasible only for few monitors; "intractable for more than four monitors"), (b) audit region given by Neyman-Pearson likelihood ratios. Likelihoods estimated by kernel density from training data. No learning of a query selector; monitors' cost and score distributions are given. Stochastic monitor calls can beat always-on. Choice among several monitors: yes (2). Conditioned on candidate actions: no.
Baselines: single-monitor class, a two-monitor policy that always calls both (Pi_BM), "naive baseline"; no random-trigger, no scene-uncertainty trigger as such (the McKenzie median-proximity heuristic is cited, not run), no oracle. Pareto: yes, safety vs budget, with seed-controlled bootstrap (Appendix A: significant "between $0.50 and $1.75").
Headline (verbatim, Abstract): "we more than double our recall rate compared to a naive baseline in a code review setting. We also show that combining two monitors can Pareto dominate using either monitor alone." Fig. 5: "Pi_SR never does worse than the other policies and achieves higher safety at some budgets".
Overlap (risk: PARTIAL on framing of which/when under a budget; NONE on method or domain). Their central statement is that the choice of which and when to call a costly monitor should be a solved optimisation rather than a heuristic.
Ours that it lacks: any state/plan conditioning (their policy depends on previous monitor scores only, not on the proposed action); perception; learned selector on features; hazards.
Baseline in MetaDrive: its "stochastic call at a fixed probability" is our random baseline at budget b; its likelihood-ratio ordering on a first-stage score is the scene-uncertainty family. Nothing new to port; cite for "stochastic mixing between operating points to trace a convex Pareto frontier" (our budget sweep can interpolate between budgets by randomising the call rate, which is a cheap way to make curves comparable at exact equal budget).
Quote: "The algorithm exhaustively searches over when and which monitors to call" (Abstract).

## 10. Learning Contextual Runtime Monitors for Safe AI-Based Autonomy, arXiv:2601.20666 (cs.LG, v3 2 Apr 2026; Luque-Cerpa et al., Chalmers/Berkeley) -- full HTML read
Domain: CARLA lane keeping/steering and a collision-avoidance variant (Scenic-generated). Code: none stated in the text.

Exact method. A "monitor" is a contextual bandit (logistic-regression or NN) whose arms are black-box CNN controllers; context = (weather, time of day, road type, distances d_c, d_p), reward = satisfaction of a safety spec (no crash, no lane exit for more than 30 steps). It learns online which controller to deploy and falls back to a fail-safe if estimated confidence is below 1-epsilon; regret bound for the LR monitor. So "monitor" here means controller selector, not perception-query selector; nothing is acquired and nothing is conditioned on candidate plans.
Baselines: weighted-average (bagging) ensemble and mixture-of-experts, with LR/NN monitors at FP tolerances 5/10/20/30%. No random, no oracle (RQ1 sanity check tests recovery of the expected controller). Budget: false-positive tolerance (rate of switching to fail-safe) acts as a safety/availability knob, but not a query budget.
Headline (verbatim, Sec. V-D): "our monitors learn to assign the best controller to each context, improving the reward by around 30% in comparison to the averaging ensemble, even when the safety controller is not used. Tolerating rates of False Positives of 5%, 10%, 20%, and 30%, ... the reward increases even further, to 80% in Scenario 1, and 70% in Scenario 2." Averaging violates the spec "in more than half of the simulations for Scenario 1, and in almost 90% of them for Scenario 2". LR monitors beat NN monitors with the same data.
Overlap (risk: NONE). Useful only as (a) a learned context-to-arm selector with a safety fallback (a pattern for our later accept/inspect/replan/fallback phase) and (b) the observation that a simple logistic model generalises better than an NN at small data, relevant to our small MLP selector.
Baseline in MetaDrive: no; but a logistic-regression selector on our geometry features is a cheap extra row (minutes).
Quote: "A monitor continuously observes the system's context and selects the controller best suited to the current conditions." (Abstract).

## 11. AdaDrive, arXiv:2511.06253 (cs.CV, 9 Nov 2025; Zhang et al., CUHK-SZ/Baidu/SYSU) -- full HTML read
Domain: language-grounded closed-loop driving, LangAuto-Tiny/Short/Long in CARLA. Code: github.com/ReaFly/AdaDrive (Abstract).

Exact method. Slow-fast: a 3M-parameter lightweight planner runs every step (fast path); a TinyLLaMA-1.1B is the slow path. "Connector-W" is an MLP on the current LS-Qformer feature, "theta_T = MLP(f'_T)", turned into a binary activation by Gumbel-Softmax. Training label is built without ground truth by a counterfactual double forward pass: "in the training stage, We perform two forward passes for trajectory prediction: one with LLM assistance yielding W_T^LLM = P(f'_T + f''_T), and another without ... Subsequently, we calculate the trajectory loss (L1 loss) for W_T^LLM and W_T ... L_ada = pi_T * L_T^LLM + (1 - pi_T) * L_T. Optimizing this objective function naturally induces pi_T = 1 when L_T^LLM < L_T and 0 otherwise" (Sec. 3.2). "Connector-H" scales the LLM contribution continuously by confidence and scene complexity. So: one expensive module, binary when-gate, learned end to end, label = with-LLM loss minus without-LLM loss (imitation L1 against expert waypoints, not a downstream decision outcome). The gate input is the current frame feature; no candidate set.
Baselines: LMDrive (3 LLMs), AD-H, and fixed-interval activation at several frequencies ("Analysis of Adaptive Collaboration", Fig. 5b,c). No scene-uncertainty trigger as such, no random (fixed interval is the periodic stand-in), no oracle.
Headline (verbatim, Table 1): LangAuto-Tiny DS 80.9 / RC 87.6 / IS 0.90 vs AD-H 68.0 / 74.4 / 0.87 and LMDrive-LLaVA 66.5; LangAuto-Short DS 70.6 vs AD-H 54.3. Table 3 ablation: without Connector 71.9 DS, with Connector-W 77.9, with both 80.9. Sec. 4: "maintaining an average activation frequency of only 0.28", "reducing GFLOPs by 62% compared to continuous activation (frequency = 1.0) while improving driving scores by 5.6% relative to the fixed-interval scheme with a similar frequency (frequency = 0.25)". Inference 189 ms vs 445-526 ms for LMDrive.
Overlap (risk: PARTIAL on the training-label recipe; NONE on candidate conditioning or tool choice). Same recipe as our expected-gain target: run with and without the expensive module, label by the difference, learn a gate on cheap features, compare against periodic activation at matched frequency, report accuracy vs. activation frequency.
Ours that it lacks: choice among tools, candidate-set conditioning, decision-level (best-candidate) score rather than waypoint L1, tool noise, oracle selector, uncertainty and random baselines at equal budget.
Baseline in MetaDrive: yes, conceptually and cheaply: a binary gate MLP on scene features trained on "gain of any tool" is the trajectory-agnostic learned gate and is a stronger version of our scene-uncertainty baseline (hours). Reproducing AdaDrive itself is not feasible (needs LMDrive stack).
Quote that collides: "By comparing LLM-assisted and LLM-free prediction during training, our model automatically identifies high-risk or complex situations where LLM intervention is most beneficial, ensuring a real on-demand activation." (Introduction).

## 12. Uncertainty-Aware Clarification in LLM Agents with Information Gain, arXiv:2606.03135 (cs.AI, 2 Jun 2026; Deng et al., HKUST-GZ) -- full HTML read
Domain: tau-Bench (retail, airline) tool-using LLM agents asking the user questions. Code: github.com/Demi-deng2/IG-clarifier (Abstract).

Exact method. A small LLM clarifier (1.7B) decides after each agent tool call whether to ask a question ("No need to ask" token) and what to ask. Reward = Bayesian belief update, Eq. (2): mean token log-probability of the ground-truth goal G* given (context, question, answer) minus the same without the exchange, "teacher-forced" by the same backbone; equals PMI between the answer and the true goal. Not asking gives reward exactly 0. Policy trained by DAPO over K on-policy sampled candidate questions with group-relative advantage; a "Strict User Simulator" prevents reward hacking. So the value signal is a per-query counterfactual gain (with vs without) measured against privileged ground truth, used as an RL reward, and it also teaches abstention. Candidates are sampled questions during training only.
Baselines: no clarification; untuned Qwen3-8B clarifier; 8B-671B LLMs as clarifiers (Table 2); LLM-as-judge reward and likelihood-only ablations; "Full User Intent" static oracle as an upper bound. No random, no uncertainty trigger. Budget: max clarification budget swept (Fig. 4): "the relationship between clarification budget and performance is clearly non-monotonic: success rates peak at intermediate budgets" (Sec. 4.3).
Headline (verbatim): Abstract "improves the success rate by 3.7% over the no-clarification baseline, while adding only 0.3 total interaction steps on average" (Table 3: average None 23.6 -> Ours 27.3, i.e. +3.7 points on a low base; Qwen3-8B clarifier -0.6). Sec. 4.1/4.2: invocations "drops sharply from 4.2 to 1.3 per task"; "our method achieves a task success rate of 17.8%, approaching the best-performing large backbone (DeepSeek-V3.1, 18.1%)"; the interactive clarifier "outperforms [the Full User Intent oracle] in the complex Airline domain (17.3% vs. 16%)". Absolute success rates are in the 10-30% range, so the differences rest on small counts.
Overlap (risk: PARTIAL on label construction; NONE otherwise). Same recipe family as our expected-gain target (value = utility with the query minus utility without, against privileged truth; zero for abstain), and the same finding that more queries can hurt ("excessive clarification introduces context pollution").
Ours that it lacks: perception tools, candidate-plan conditioning, a ranker/oracle-best-candidate accuracy, equal-budget comparison with a trajectory-agnostic trigger, noise levels.
Baseline in MetaDrive: no as a method (RL over free text). Idea to borrow: the abstain reward is exactly 0 and the mean reward over K samples, which in our table is "gain of the abstain action = 0"; the selector can be trained with a softmax over tools including a zero-gain abstain arm.
Quote that collides: "the agent intervenes only when asking a question is expected to reduce uncertainty about the latent user goal and improve downstream tool use" (Introduction).

## Corrections to the listing-sweep entries (raw/M*.md)
- AS-DDTO (2606.22277): M2 lists it as HIGH "idea only" and says the value of a query depends on "where candidate trajectories diverge". In the full text the candidates are goal locations to identify, the information term is a distance-based surrogate (phi(d)=d or d^2) that pulls the shared trunk closer to candidates, and the divergence of trajectories is what DDTO itself maximises to delay commitment. It is a weaker precedent than the abstract suggests: downgrade to LOW. Its conformal use (Fisher-combined p-values, nested candidate sets) is only background for our calibration phase.
- CTD (2604.14251) and To Call or Not to Call (2605.00737): M4's HIGH is right, but note that CTD's label depends on the true class and both models' outputs (so it is a gain label like ours), and that 2605.00737's utility estimator, the analogue of our selector, "does not reliably predict" utility; only the need estimator helps. Both are binary, single-tool.
- Proof-of-Perception (2603.00324): M4 calls it a scene-uncertainty-style trigger; confirmed (conformal set size and a learned controller), with a real budget Pareto. Its baseline numbers are the authors' own runs.
- IG-clarifier (2606.03135): M7's "+3.7%" is absolute points on a base of 23.6% average success.
- PRAM-R (2603.04222): "comparable trajectory accuracy" is ADE 1.013 -> 1.124 on 10 scenes.

## Novelty statement
Across these twelve full-text reads, plus the nine in X1/X2, no paper chooses among several perception tools for a driving planner conditioned on how K candidate trajectories diverge, and none evaluates that choice as decision accuracy against query budget with scene-uncertainty, random and oracle selectors; the closest new work either gates one expensive call (CTD, To Call or Not to Call, AdaDrive), chooses among tools from the node's own uncertainty (Proof-of-Perception, #8), or optimises information with a hand-built term in a non-driving setting (AS-DDTO, Active Probing). What these papers add to the prior statements (DEEP, ETM, SIGMA, Learning When to Search, and 2611.06702 as the closest plan-set-conditioned precedent) is that we must also treat three things as prior art: training a value head on with-minus-without gain labels (CTD, 2605.00737, AdaDrive, IG-clarifier), showing that uncertainty is a poor proxy for the benefit of a call and that some calls hurt (CTD, 2605.00737), and calibrating a threshold on the learned signal with finite-sample guarantees under a budget (CTD's LTT, PoP's split conformal). The claim that remains is narrow and defensible: a selector given the divergence of the candidate set picks among heterogeneous perception tools and abstain better than the same learner without candidate features and than scene-uncertainty triggers at equal query budget, in hazard-injected MetaDrive with controlled tool noise and an oracle selector. The warning from 2605.00737 (utility is much harder to learn than need) means this must be shown with a matched ablation and nDG or harm reported next to accuracy; it also gives us the strongest wording of why candidate geometry should help, since it supplies the missing information about what the tool would reveal. All twelve are full-text reads, but the claim is still an absence-of-prior-work statement over the papers we found, not over the field.
