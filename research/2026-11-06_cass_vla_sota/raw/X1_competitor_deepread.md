## DEEP: "The Decision Value of Perception Compute" (arXiv 2609.35910; OR l9QJO8BIoE)

Authors: Hoang Pham Cong, Ho Viet Duc Luong (HUST, Hanoi). cs.LG, 2026-09-28. Submitted to ICLR 2027 (OpenReview primary area: datasets and benchmarks). Full text read.

Exact method
- Setting: input x with reference scene state; a cheap mode and a full mode each produce a perception output; a fixed downstream decision rule with loss L. "We define the decision value of escalating input x as (1)" = loss(cheap) minus loss(full); positive when the full mode helps, negative when it hurts. (Equation symbols were stripped in the HTML text; wording from section 3: "which is positive when the full mode reduces downstream loss, zero when it has no effect, and negative when it increases the loss.")
- Oracle: with budget B, "the oracle selects" the B most valuable escalations and, since values can be negative, "may leave capacity unused". Normalized decision gain nDG = fraction of oracle value realized. Harm ratio characterizes the perception-decision pair. Proposition 1 gives the conditions for a score to be optimal: rank positive-value inputs first in decreasing order of value, then zero before negative.
- Allocators must be pre-escalation: "an allocator commits before the expensive mode runs and may use only the input, the cheap output, previous frames, calibration and ego proprioception". Allocator's own compute is charged under latency/energy budgets. Counterfactual evaluation is cached, so new allocators need only their scores.
- Tracks: core = YOLOv8s at 320 vs 640 px (plus other pairs on KITTI), braking controller, receding-horizon controller, and a published learned planner (the PKL planner) on nuScenes; external = PDM-Closed and IDM open-loop on nuPlan with real detector outcomes. "DEEP measures single-step counterfactual value; closed-loop evaluation from camera input needs sensor simulation."
- Allocators tested (section 4.3): "random routing and ego speed as simple controls; cheap-detection uncertainty and cheap-side criticality as hand-designed signals; ridge and gradient-boosted gates over cheap-side features; a detection-list router in the style of ORIC ...; and a 0.15 GFLOPs pixel router".

What it does that our project also claims (novelty risk: PARTIAL, on framing and evaluation, none on the candidate-conditioned part)
- Targets defined by realized downstream decision loss, not perception quality (our "expected gain" targets).
- Budget-constrained decision oracle as reference, random and uncertainty baselines, accuracy-vs-budget style evaluation (our Pareto + oracle selector).
- Shows scene/cheap-side uncertainty is no better than random, which is our central baseline claim, but from the perception-compute side.
- Includes a published planner (PDM-Closed) and an open-loop evaluation, like ours.

What our project does that DEEP does not
- Choice among heterogeneous tools (ROI/occlusion, depth, collision check) plus abstain; DEEP has a single binary escalation (and fidelity levels of the same detector).
- Conditioning on the K candidate trajectories (how they diverge). DEEP's allocator inputs contain no candidate set; the decision rule is fixed and hidden from the allocator.
- Hazard-injected scenarios with controlled tool-noise levels, tool as information source rather than a better pass of the same sensor.
- Ranker over candidates and oracle best candidate; VLM features; later calibrated accept/inspect/replan.

Baseline in MetaDrive? Yes as a protocol, not as code (no code found). Easy to hard: reimplementing the metric set (decision value per tool per scene, nDG, harm ratio, selected-harm, paired bootstrap by scene, charging selector cost) is easy because our expected-gain table already holds per-tool per-scene gains. Their allocator families (ridge/GBM/MLP over cheap-side features, uncertainty, ego speed, criticality) can be added as additional "trajectory-agnostic" baselines in about a day. Their ORIC-style detection-list router is perception-specific and not applicable.

Collisions with our claim (verbatim, with section)
- Abstract: "Because this value can be negative, the allocation of perception compute should be judged against a budget-constrained decision oracle, with uniform full-fidelity inference as a baseline rather than an upper bound."
- Section 1: "What ultimately determines whether additional compute is worthwhile is therefore its effect on the downstream decision, for which perception gain is only an indirect signal."
- Section 5.3: "Neither heuristic significantly outperforms random in any cell at the 20% selection budget; cheap-side criticality is significantly worse on three cells and uncertainty on two."
- Section 5.3 on training targets: "Trained instead on the published routing objectives of ORIC and budget-adaptive routing, the same five router architectures beat random in 12 and 15 of 200 architecture, cell and budget combinations, against 40 for decision value". This supports training on decision-value targets (our expected gain).

The two findings you flagged, quoted exactly
1. Learned allocators select nearly as much harm as random.
   - Abstract: "Learned allocators recover part of the oracle's value by finding beneficial escalations but select nearly as much harm as random, and once their own computation is charged at a 20% latency budget, only the lightweight routers, at about 3.5% of a full detector pass, still beat random."
   - Section 5.5: "Learned allocators beat random by capturing more benefit but select nearly as much harm (Figure 3 a); ego speed has the largest net gain on average, carried by the KITTI cells."
   - Section 6: "at a 20% selection budget they capture on average about a third of the oracle's beneficial value and select nearly as much harm as random, and under measured budgets only low-overhead ones keep an advantage over random."
   - Section 5.5: "For learned allocators that fit a measured budget, ranking, not overhead or causal enforcement, accounts for most of the gap".
2. Perception gain disagrees in sign with decision value.
   - Abstract: "On nuScenes, perception-level gain frequently disagrees in sign with decision value. This mismatch has practical consequences: choosing among fixed deployable signals by missed-object perception gain rather than by decision value reduces realized test decision gain by 7.4% of the all-cheap loss on average."
   - Section 5.1: "the signs disagree on 25-33% of braking and 50-51% of planner frames where both are non-zero." (four perception-level gains vs decision value). Section 2 says "25-51%".
   - Section 5.4: "the perception and decision objectives share no optimum in 43 of 52 cell-budget pairs."
   - Harm headline (abstract): "34-54% of the escalations that change downstream loss make it worse".

What this implies for our selector
- Our "expected gain" training target is the right target, and DEEP is independent evidence for it (decision-value labels beat perception-gain labels, 40 vs 12 and 15 of 200 combos). Use the sign-aware framing in related work.
- The risk is real: a learned selector can look good on mean gain yet pick almost as many harmful queries as random. Our evaluation must report, next to accuracy vs budget, (a) benefit captured, (b) harm selected (queries whose tool output makes the ranker's pick worse than no tool), (c) nDG vs oracle, and a no-tool/abstain comparison. A claim that the candidate-conditioned selector beats scene-uncertainty at equal budget must be shown on harm as well as gain.
- Give the selector an explicit abstain threshold at predicted gain < 0 (their oracle "may leave capacity unused"); check whether our expected-gain targets can be negative under tool noise (noisy depth/ROI can flip a correct ranking) and whether the MLP predicts the sign. DEEP's Proposition 1 says quality depends on the boundary between beneficial and non-beneficial, not only on ranking among beneficial ones; our MLP is trained on regression targets, so evaluate the sign boundary explicitly.
- Expect scene-uncertainty to be near random, and a simple trajectory-agnostic feature (like DEEP's ego speed) to be a strong trivial baseline. Add ego speed and a gradient-boosted gate on the same geometry features without candidate-divergence features as the control; the claim "candidate conditioning helps" needs that matched ablation (same learner, features with vs without divergence).
- Charge the selector's own cost (ours is an MLP on cached features, so cost is negligible; state it). Use paired bootstrap over scenes, not frames, as DEEP does.
- DEEP's benchmark is explicitly open-loop single-step, so our open-loop framing is not a weakness relative to it, but we should not claim closed-loop benefit.

## MosaicDrive (OR 9I96DzTfHb) - abstract only

Exact method: only what the abstract says. "derives camera and tool requirements from the question, requires the selected evidence before producing an answer, and resolves the resulting observations through Arbiter, a source-aware verifier that checks claims against available evidence, issues targeted re-queries, and preserves conflicts that cannot be settled." Evaluated on MOSICQA, "a multi-camera driving question-answering resource with executed tool traces and scene-disjoint splits". Keywords: active perception, tool-using agents. TLDR: "combines task-aware camera acquisition with evidence verification to ground driving decisions in complementary views and identify unresolved conflicts."

Overlap (novelty risk: PARTIAL, on motivation only): it attacks the same weakness we attack.
- Abstract: "Tool-augmented driving agents typically acquire additional camera views when they are uncertain about what they have already seen. We identify a structural limitation of this design: the confidence that triggers retrieval is computed from evidence the agent already holds, and so carries no information about regions it has never observed."
- Abstract: "a newly retrieved observation can contradict or override a correct prior judgment, so broader acquisition is not monotonically beneficial." This parallels DEEP's negative decision value and our noisy-tool case.
- Conditioning is on the question (driving QA), not on candidate plans; no planner or ranker in the loop as far as the abstract shows (UNKNOWN beyond abstract).
- Ours that it lacks (as far as the abstract shows): candidate-trajectory conditioning, oracle best-candidate scoring, injected hazards, a budget Pareto against uncertainty/random/oracle (its protocol "separates ... answer quality from acquisition cost", so a cost axis exists but no sweep is stated).
- Baseline in MetaDrive: hard. It needs a VLM agent, multi-camera views (MetaDrive can render several cameras but our front-camera-only pipeline would need extension), and a verifier; no code. Treat as a conceptual comparison and borrow its evaluation split (coverage vs selectivity, correction vs abstention, quality vs cost). Its "uncertainty trigger is blind to unobserved regions" argument is directly usable to motivate why our occlusion-ROI tool needs a non-uncertainty trigger.

## ETM: Event Trigger Module (OR oa4EWvJlLC) - abstract only

Exact method (abstract): "a lightweight event-driven policy that selectively activates the VLM only when refreshed semantic reasoning is expected to improve downstream planning. We formulate VLM invocation as an information-gain-driven decision that explicitly balances semantic utility against computational cost." Inputs: "short-term visual dynamics, long-term temporal context, and compact motion-aware scalar cues". Training: "a hybrid supervision strategy that combines corner-case trigger labels with information gain based pseudo-labels". nuScenes result: "reducing the VLM invocation rate from 100% to 32%", "decreases inference latency by nearly 50% and lowers computational cost by approximately 64%", "comparable planning accuracy to always-triggered VLM systems".

Overlap (risk: PARTIAL, on the learning-from-gain-labels recipe): learned selector trained on labels that measure planning improvement from the expensive call, which is the same recipe as our expected-gain targets. Differences: binary gate on a whole VLM call; no choice among tools; inputs are temporal scene features, not the candidate set; pseudo-label construction details are UNKNOWN from the abstract (cannot tell whether labels use the planner's output per candidate).
Ours that it lacks: tool choice, candidate-divergence conditioning, hazard injection with noise levels, oracle selector, trajectory-agnostic vs candidate-conditioned ablation.
Baseline in MetaDrive: moderate. A "learned binary trigger on scene features trained on gain labels" is just our MLP with candidate features removed and a single "any tool" output; we can build that ETM-style baseline ourselves in hours, named "ETM-style trigger" (not a reproduction, state so). No code found.
Collision quotes (abstract): "when should the expensive VLM be invoked?" and "information gain based pseudo-labels".

## Certifying the Information Value of Adaptive Tool Use (OR JUWtWs4Tgb) - abstract only

Exact method (abstract): defines "adaptive information value as the improvement in optimal expected task utility from choosing queries based on previous responses rather than fixing them in advance, under the same task and query budget", and derives "a sound finite-budget certificate that upper-bounds optimal adaptive performance without enumerating adaptive policies", combining "pairwise response-channel overlap with utility-sensitive fractional matching", with "heterogeneous query costs, multivalued responses, and memoryless noise". Experiments: controlled network diagnosis; "63 selected topology lineages and three language-model families"; "externally optimized adaptive evidence improves prediction accuracy by 5.84 percentage points on the positive-gap interface, with a 4.37-point larger gain than on a matched zero-gap control". Negative finding: "when the models select their own queries, adaptive querying underperforms pre-committed querying in our tests, distinguishing the ability to use adaptive evidence from the ability to select it."

Overlap (risk: none on the driving claim; PARTIAL on theory vocabulary): general value-of-information theory for tool querying with heterogeneous costs and noise. No driving, no candidate plans, no budget Pareto against uncertainty triggers. Its value is conceptual: (i) an upper-bound/certificate idea for how much any selector can gain given a tool interface (our oracle selector is the empirical analogue); (ii) support for our finding that prompting the VLM to pick the tool fails ("distinguishing the ability to use adaptive evidence from the ability to select it").
Note: it concerns adaptive (sequential, response-dependent) querying; our setting is a single non-sequential query, so its certificate does not directly apply (our selector is "pre-committed" in its terminology). Do not over-claim the link.
Baseline in MetaDrive: not applicable (theory + benchmark audit).

## DriveScout (OR rXDM54aQdX) - abstract only

Exact method (abstract): "a reinforcement learning framework that formulates visual focusing as selective evidence acquisition. DriveScout combines Target-Grounded Spatial Credit with Necessity-Aware Focus Optimization, while a Progressive Blur Curriculum supports learning as the visual evidence becomes increasingly degraded." Benchmark SCOUT: "a paired driving VQA benchmark built from driving scenes in nuScenes, where each clear observation is matched with motion-degraded variants for small-target understanding." Claim: "improves robustness to blur while reducing redundant visual operations." Keywords: Tool-Augmented Reasoning.

Overlap (risk: PARTIAL, on "selective acquisition" wording): DriveAgent-R1-style learned decision whether to crop; "indiscriminate image operations add inference cost" is the same cost argument as ours. It is VQA on small-target recognition, a single operation type (crop/zoom), no planner, no candidate trajectories, no tool choice among sensor types, trigger conditioned on the image and question. Budget curves: not stated.
Baseline in MetaDrive: hard and not meaningful. It needs a Think-with-Images MLLM trained with RL on VQA; no code. Use as a representative of the "RL zoom agent" family in related work only.

## Novelty statement

As of this read, none of DEEP, MosaicDrive, ETM, Certifying or DriveScout (nor SIGMA) conditions the choice of perception query on the set of candidate plans: DEEP's allocators and ETM's trigger see scene/cheap-side features, MosaicDrive and DriveScout condition on the question or image, SIGMA conditions on one planner's response to scene realizations, and Certifying has no plans at all. Our project can therefore still claim, with the caveat that four of these are abstract-only, that (1) the selection signal is how the K candidate trajectories diverge, tested against a matched trajectory-agnostic ablation at equal budget; (2) the choice is among several heterogeneous tools plus abstain, rather than a binary escalate/invoke; (3) the evaluation is an open-loop decision-accuracy vs query-budget Pareto in hazard-injected MetaDrive with controlled tool-noise levels and an oracle selector. What we cannot claim as new: that decision-level gain beats uncertainty or perception gain as a trigger and that harmful acquisition exists (DEEP, SIGMA, MosaicDrive), or that gain-derived pseudo-labels can train a learned trigger (ETM). Given DEEP, we should report harm-selected and nDG alongside accuracy, and treat "learned selector beats random by a large margin" as unproven until the sign boundary is evaluated.

### DynaChain: "Closed-Loop Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Vision-Language Driving Agents"
- Exact method (abstract): "We formalise reasoning-chain selection as a closed-loop Markov decision process"; DynaChain "decides at every frame which questions to ask before committing to an action", trained "with closed-loop agentic reinforcement learning, under a hybrid advantage that combines the episode's driving outcome with a dense per-step reward derived from the benchmark's scoring rule". Details of the action space and state input: UNKNOWN beyond the abstract.
- Collides with our claim (risk PARTIAL, on "selecting what to look up is a decision whose value is realized downstream"):
  - Abstract: "which questions a frame warrants is itself a decision, and a fixed chain never makes it."
  - Abstract: "the value of a question is realised only in the trajectory that follows."
  - Abstract: "the gain comes from which questions the agent asks rather than how accurately it answers them, and that it requires both learned selection and closed-loop training."
  - Abstract: "a model answering this chain drives worse than one answering nothing" (acquisition can hurt, like DEEP).
- What ours does that it does not (as far as the abstract shows): the options are perception tools with controlled noise, not VLM reasoning questions; candidate-trajectory conditioning; an explicit equal-budget Pareto against scene-uncertainty, random and an oracle selector; a cheap supervised selector instead of closed-loop RL. Its strong point against us: it is closed loop with a real driving score; ours is open-loop decision accuracy, so a reviewer may ask why not closed loop.
- Baseline in MetaDrive: hard. It needs RL training of a VLM agent on Bench2Drive-VL (CARLA); no code yet. Cite as the closest "learned selection of what to ask" competitor and as evidence that selection matters more than answer accuracy.

### Learning When to Search (generative motion planning, nuPlan closed loop)
- Exact method (abstract): "a lightweight history-aware model to decide whether search should be performed after the base trajectory is generated. If search is needed, the original search module generates and evaluates additional candidates; otherwise, the base trajectory is used directly." Claim: "the benefit of search varies greatly across planning steps and is related to both the current planning information and recent history."
- Collides (risk PARTIAL, nearest on the "gate by realized planning benefit with a Pareto vs fixed schedules" evaluation): the budget-vs-score comparison (86.07 at 56.81% vs 85.64 at 100%, and "performs better than fixed search schedules at similar search rates"). It gates candidate generation (compute), not perception tools, is binary, and the gate sees the base trajectory but not a candidate set (candidates are generated only if search runs). Whether it uses candidate divergence: UNKNOWN.
- Ours that it lacks: perception-tool choice, noise levels, injected hazards, scene-uncertainty baseline (not stated), candidate-set-conditioned features.
- Baseline in MetaDrive: moderate as a "history/base-trajectory gate" variant only; we have no history-based generative planner. Idea to borrow: fixed-schedule baselines at matched rate (add periodic/every-n-steps selector as a baseline).

### Foresight on Demand (FD-WAM)
- Exact method (abstract): "Utility-Aware Selective Foresight trains a router with the realized planning score difference between the same planner's foresight and no-foresight paths, enabling selective prediction from observed history alone." Plus a "Decision-Relevant Foresight Representation". Abstract motivation: "Foresight can waste computation when history already supports a reliable plan, and can even degrade planning when its predictions are inaccurate."
- Collides (risk PARTIAL, closest in training target to our expected-gain labels): a router supervised by the realized planner-score difference with vs without the extra information source, and the admission that the extra source can degrade planning (same point as DEEP). It is binary, uses history only (explicitly not the candidate set), and gates a model's own prediction branch, not external perception tools.
- Ours that it lacks: tool choice, candidate conditioning, noise levels, oracle selector and budget Pareto (not stated in abstract).
- Baseline in MetaDrive: easy as an idea, the same "gain-supervised binary gate" baseline as ETM: our MLP, any-tool output, no candidate features, labels = score difference with vs without tool.

### Updated Novelty statement (replaces the one above)
Across DEEP, ETM, SIGMA, Learning When to Search, Foresight on Demand, DynaChain, MosaicDrive, DriveScout and Certifying, the field has converged on three points we must therefore treat as prior work, not as our contribution: gating an expensive module by realized downstream planning gain (ETM, FD-WAM, Learning When to Search, SIGMA), showing that scene or perception uncertainty is a poor trigger (DEEP, SIGMA, MosaicDrive), and showing that extra evidence or computation can hurt (DEEP, MosaicDrive, DynaChain, FD-WAM). What none of them does, on abstracts and the DEEP full text, is condition the query decision on the set of candidate plans, how they diverge, while also choosing among several heterogeneous perception tools with an abstain option; the nearest are Learning When to Search (gate sees the base trajectory, binary, compute not perception) and DynaChain (selects among questions, closed-loop RL, no candidates). Our remaining claims are therefore (1) a matched ablation showing that candidate-divergence features beat the same learner without them and beat scene-uncertainty at equal query budget, (2) tool choice plus abstain under controlled tool noise with an oracle selector, and (3) a Pareto on hazard-injected MetaDrive with harm-selected and nDG reported next to accuracy. Seven of the nine papers are abstract-only, so these absence-of-prior-work statements need a re-check once arXiv versions or PDFs become readable. Honest weaknesses to state: open-loop only (DynaChain, Learning When to Search and DEEP-style benchmarks differ in being closed loop or counterfactual-cached), and DEEP's warning that learned gates may select nearly as much harm as random.
