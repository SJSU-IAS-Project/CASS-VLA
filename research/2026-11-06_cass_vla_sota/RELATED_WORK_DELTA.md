# Related-work delta for the literature survey

This note tells the team what to change in `docs/literature_survey.md` (and in two positioning documents) after the literature scan of 2026-11-06. It has four parts: claims that the scan weakens, new rows for the summary table, replacement "SOTA observations", and a rewritten "Gap addressed by this project".

## Changes after the audit

This note was first written before `AUDIT.md` existed. It has since been reconciled with `AUDIT.md` and the revised `REPORT.md` (section 8). What changed:

- Removed the claim that no paper varies the information given to a scorer. RRDrive does (its Table 5 compares geometry, risk or both). The claim is now that no paper chooses, per decision, which information to acquire.
- BLUE and ASSCG moved from the "how much to think" group to the decision-value group. That group has seven papers; ETM is close but its labels measure information gain. BLUE became a table row, because it also says that scene complexity and kinematic features struggle to predict when language helps.
- WOD-E2E has rater scores on 3 trajectories per example, chosen from fewer than 12. It lost its table row and is now a weak real-data match, listed in the cite-in-text paragraph.
- DEEP wording now says learned allocators capture more benefit than random but select nearly as much harm. The 34 to 54% figure is limited to DEEP's monocular-geometry settings.
- The prompting evidence no longer says prompting is "known to work poorly". None of the cited sources tests a VLM choosing a perception tool. DriveJudge, When2Tool and the Certifying submission are described as related, not as direct evidence.
- Illusion and ToolGate findings were narrowed to what each paper measured. The RRDrive rank of 33.9 is labelled as the baseline scorer. SIGMA's baselines are local-only and fixed-period. DriveBench's black-image result covers "a significant portion of models".
- The novelty claim and gap section now follow the current `REPORT.md` section 1, with its five limits on our own results.
- Added Best-of-Evidence (arXiv:2607.20950, also OpenReview U1JptdfbDT; read as abstract only) as the nearest candidate-conditioned query idea outside driving. It replaced the Drive by Hindsight row to keep the table at 20 rows.
- The seven abstract-only submissions are now named below.

## Read this first

Status of the evidence:

- `AUDIT.md` exists and rated the first draft of `REPORT.md`; this note follows the revised `REPORT.md`. If the two ever disagree, trust `AUDIT.md`.
- "No paper does X" always means "no paper we found does X". Seven close OpenReview submissions were read as abstracts only: FD-WAM, Learning When to Search, ETM, DynaChain, MosaicDrive, DriveScout and "Certifying the Information Value of Adaptive Tool Use" (Best-of-Evidence was also abstract only). Those rows say "abstract only".
- OpenReview submissions are marked "under review". Do not cite their numbers as established results.
- Quotes in Part 1 are copied from the source files, so they keep the source's dashes, asterisks and bold marks.

The one-paragraph version: the broad idea of deciding whether to acquire information by its effect on the plan, instead of by scene uncertainty, is no longer new. SIGMA, DEEP, ETM, Foresight on Demand, Learning When to Search and the off-road paper 2611.06702 all do some version of it. Several papers also show that extra evidence can hurt. What we still did not find is a driving selector that chooses among several perception tools using how the candidate trajectories diverge, and scores that choice as accuracy against query budget next to scene-uncertainty, random and oracle selectors. The documents should claim that, and only that.

The example phrase "these approaches generally do not provide ..." does not appear in the three documents, so it is not listed below.

## Part 3. Updated "SOTA observations"

Replace the current six bullets with the following. Items 1 and 2 are unchanged except for two names.

Recent work establishes several important capabilities:

1. VLA models can generate interpretable reasoning traces and driving actions.
2. Counterfactual reasoning can improve planning by allowing the model to revise unsafe behavior before execution.
3. Critics and verifiers can score and refine candidate trajectories (Judge, Then Drive, DriveJudge, DriveVer). Candidate scorers keep improving, and a large gap to the best candidate remains. The best candidate in the top-K of DriveSuprim rises from 91.9 PDMS at K=1 to 98.7 at K=256. In RRDrive the oracle-best candidate ranks 33.9th on average under the baseline scorer, and 27.7th after the scorer is given a risk profile for each candidate.
4. Tool-using driving agents have multiplied (DriveAgent-R1, AgentThink, MTRDrive, OmniDrive-R1, Drive by Hindsight and Foresight). None that we found conditions the tool choice on the candidate trajectories or reports accuracy against a query budget. Drive by Hindsight and Foresight has 27 tools and calls at least one on 99.5% of queries.
5. Tool calls are not automatically useful. The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use audits six models and finds two failure modes. In one, the returned observation has no causal effect on the answer, and the gain is concentrated in a calibrated minority of calls. In ToolGate, 78.3% of calls leave the immediate answer unchanged and 9.9% change it from right to wrong.
6. Many adaptive driving systems decide how much to think, not what to look at (AdaThinkDrive, AdaDrive, CAR-VLA). None of them chooses a perception query.
7. Triggers are moving from uncertainty to decision value. SIGMA, DEEP, FD-WAM, Learning When to Search, ETM, the off-road paper 2611.06702, BLUE and ASSCG score a query or a mode switch by its effect on the plan. BLUE reports that scene complexity and kinematic features struggle to predict when language helps. Outside driving, Calibration Is Not Control lowers regret from 0.513 to 0.094 on ALFWorld by changing only the training target to intervention value. Training on the gain of a query is established practice (Calibrate-Then-Delegate, To Call or Not to Call, AdaDrive).
8. More evidence can hurt, and learned selectors are imperfect. In DEEP's monocular-geometry settings, 34% to 54% of escalations that change the loss make it worse. Learned allocators capture more benefit than random, about a third of the oracle's beneficial value, but select nearly as much harm as random. FD-WAM, MosaicDrive and DynaChain report that extra information or computation can degrade results.
9. Adding information to a candidate scorer is already studied: RRDrive compares scorers that see geometry, risk or both. The one precedent we found that chooses a sensing action from a planner's candidate set is the off-road paper 2611.06702, which is rule-based and not a driving system. We did not find a per-decision choice of which information to acquire, under a budget, in driving.
10. Sensor degradation and long-tail hazards can cause reasoning instability and dangerous planning errors (Lost in Fog). A planner can also perceive a hazard and still fail to react: in Perceives but Doesn't Brake, hazard presence is decoded at AUC 0.998 while the planner still intrudes. A tool helps only if its output can change the ranker's choice.
11. Frozen VLM features add little over simple geometry (Auditing Latent-Space Monitors). Related work points the same way as our weak prompted-VLM result, but none of it tests a VLM choosing a perception tool: DriveJudge scores trajectories, When2Tool studies text-only agents, and the Certifying submission studies language models on network diagnosis.
12. Conformal and risk-calibrated methods exist for driving monitors (Localized Conformal Safety Monitoring), for budgeted evidence acquisition (Look Again Before You Abstain) and for tool-using agents (Proof-of-Perception, Calibrate-Then-Delegate). In the papers we found, they are not combined with a driving planner that also chooses among perception tools.
13. Evaluation has limits. Offline metrics correlate poorly with closed-loop results (Scalable Offline Metrics). No benchmark we found covers occluded or ambiguous traffic lights. For real data, WOD-E2E has rater scores on only 3 trajectories per example, so it is a weak match for "K candidates with a best one".

```markdown
## Gap addressed by this project

Prior work has answered several nearby questions:

- Can a VLA produce a better trajectory, or reflect on one it has produced?
- Can a critic or verifier score and re-rank candidate trajectories?
- Can a driving agent call a visual tool, and how much should it think before acting?
- Can a system decide whether an extra query or step is worth its cost by what it does to the plan, instead of by how uncertain the scene looks?
- Can a risk monitor or a call threshold be calibrated?

The fourth question is no longer open. SIGMA, DEEP, ETM, Foresight on Demand, Learning When to Search, BLUE and ASSCG all score an expensive step by its effect on planning, and the off-road paper 2611.06702 does so for a planner's candidate rollouts. Several of these papers also show that extra evidence can hurt and that scene uncertainty is a weak trigger. Training a value head on the gain of a query is also established. We cite this work as prior art and do not claim it.

In the papers we found, one question remains open. Existing triggers make a single call-or-not decision, or choose among sensing actions without learning, outside driving. RRDrive shows that giving a scorer more information about each candidate helps, but it does not choose which information to acquire. We did not find a driving system that chooses among several perception tools from how a planner's candidate trajectories diverge, and none that scores that choice as accuracy against a query budget next to scene-uncertainty, random and oracle selectors. This is an absence over the papers we found, not over the field. Seven close OpenReview submissions could be read only as abstracts.

PlanDiscrim, the first phase of CASS-VLA, asks this question:

> We study which perception query a driving planner should make, given its candidate trajectories. A learned selector chooses among ROI inspection, depth and a collision check, or abstains. It uses features of how the candidates diverge and is trained on each tool's decision gain. Averaged over query budgets, it beats the same learner without candidate features, ranker margin and random selection. It is evaluated in hazard-injected MetaDrive at three tool-noise levels, against an oracle selector and a fair ceiling.

The biggest open risk is the size of the candidate-conditioning gain. Our own results show five limits:

- The selector leads the scene-only MLP by only 1 to 2 points on average.
- It falls behind that rival at 0.95 and 1.00 calls per decision at mid noise, and at 1.00 with high noise or blocked ROI.
- It falls behind on `occluded_cross_traffic` on the final split at half a call per decision (0.330 against 0.347). With 510 seeds on the test split, averaged over budgets, it leads there (0.297 against 0.272).
- The features that describe how the candidates diverge changed the logistic selector by less than 0.01.
- The headline table uses single-draw targets. With expected-gain targets and 510 seeds, the lead grows in four of six cells.

DEEP also reports that learned allocators capture more benefit than random but select nearly as much harm as random, so we report harm selected and normalized decision gain next to accuracy. The evaluation is open loop, and offline metrics correlate poorly with closed-loop results.

Calibrated accept, veto, replan and fallback decisions are later phases of CASS-VLA. They are not part of this claim. We expect to start from Localized Conformal Safety Monitoring and Look Again Before You Abstain, and we will describe them as contributions only after we build and test them.
```
