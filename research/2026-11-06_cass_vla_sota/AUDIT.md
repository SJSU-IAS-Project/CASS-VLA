## WRONG and OVERSTATED items, most serious first

1. **#48 WRONG.** "No paper we found varies the information given to the scorer, which is the project's question." RRDrive 2609.21486, cited in the same bullet, does exactly that: candidate-conditioned risk queries, plus Table 5 comparing geometry, risk, or both. Narrow the claim (no per-decision choice among tools), or the gap statement fails on first read.
2. **#39 WRONG.** BLUE and ASSCG are called "how much to think" systems that belong with the scene-uncertainty baselines. BLUE trains its gate on realized success-rate gaps between language and direct modes and reports that scene-complexity and kinematic features struggle to predict it. That is a decision-value trigger and a direct competitor to the "scene-only" baseline. ASSCG trains by GRPO on trajectory-score rewards. Both belong with the six decision-value papers in 3.1, which then becomes eight.
3. **#60 WRONG.** WOD-E2E rater scores cover three trajectories per example, picked from a pool of fewer than 12, not "up to 64". The "nearest real-data match to K candidates with an oracle best" recommendation loses most of its force.
4. **#72, #73 OVERSTATED (own results).** The novelty statement and "already cover the core comparison" omit that the selector falls behind the scene-only MLP at the top budgets (0.95, 1.00 mid noise; 1.00 high/blocked ROI) and on `occluded_cross_traffic`, that trajectory-feature groups moved the logistic selector under 0.01, that the top shuffle drops are scene features, and that the headline table uses single-draw, not expected-gain, targets. #5: the "10 points on occluded_pedestrian" is 4.8 points in the later 510-seed table.
5. **#20 / section 6 heading OVERSTATED.** DEEP does not say learned allocators barely beat random; it says they "beat random by capturing more benefit but select nearly as much harm", capturing about a third of the oracle's beneficial value. #22: 34-54% holds for monocular-geometry settings only.
6. **#44, #45 OVERSTATED.** Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: no causal effect is one of two failure modes, with a calibrated minority carrying the gain. ToolGate: 78.3% leave the immediate forced-answer argmax unchanged, while 9.9% flip it wrongly.
7. **#35, #49, #50 OVERSTATED (evidence does not match the heading).** "Prompting a VLM to choose the tool is known to work poorly" rests on a network-diagnosis LLM paper, a trajectory-scoring AUC (where tools help, 50.06 to 56.13) and a text-only LLM benchmark. None tests a VLM choosing a perception tool.
8. **#34 WRONG (internal inconsistency).** "All except DEEP are abstract-only" refers to five papers that do not include DEEP; the seven abstract-only papers in section 1 are never named.
9. **#67 WRONG.** papers.csv has 50 HIGH rows plus one blank, not 51.
10. **#47, #61, #62 OVERSTATED (scope).** RRDrive 33.9 is the baseline scorer; BOKBO's warning is specific to noise-perturbed K-sampling; Value-of-Intervention's heterogeneity is temporal dispersion in VLA scheduling, not hazard-family heterogeneity.
11. **#14, #25, #43, #56, #69 minor.** SIGMA has local-only plus fixed-period baselines; HybridDriveVLA code link is announced and 404; "only DriveAgent-R1" untested for DriveScout; DriveBench is "a significant portion of models"; scan window end 04:00 not reached; DriveSuprim (June 2025) sits under "since mid-2026".
